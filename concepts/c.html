Universities produce the science of limits. Do they live within them?

Planetary Universities measures whether universities treat environmental limits as boundaries to work within, or as goals to trade away.

Australian public universities 2016 to 2025 Read the argument

7 of 9 planetary boundaries are breached. Planetary Health Check 2025.

01

A ranking that adds up the wrong things.

Australian universities report against the Sustainable Development Goals, and are ranked on them. The Times Higher Education Impact Rankings add the goals together as if one can offset another. For the four environmental goals, clean water, climate, life below water and life on land, that logic fails. They mark ecological limits. Breaching one cannot be made up by scoring well elsewhere.

A ranking built this way contradicts the science universities themselves produce.

That contradiction matters most for universities. Their authority rests on the science they produce. It is hard to sustain when the institutions that identify ecological limits do not recognise those limits in their own conduct.

02

A goal can be traded. A limit cannot.

Existing keyword methods find where a goal is mentioned. They cannot tell a footprint pledge from a carbon budget. The Instrument codes every genuine mention on a four-level ladder.

0 Silent
Named, but not framed. "Our commitment to addressing climate change."
1 Aspirational
A direction with no measure. "Another step towards reducing our carbon footprint."
2 Quantified
A target with a number and a date. "Net zero scope 1 and scope 2 emissions by 2030."
3 Limit
A commitment the university must stay within. "We will operate within our fair share of the remaining carbon budget."

A keyword count treats all four as one mention. The Instrument tells them apart.

03

The tool finds. The researcher judges.

The Planetary Universities Instrument is a coding method with a text-analysis tool built in Document Lens. Automation locates passages, allocates them to an SDG and removes duplicates. Coders then judge relevance, framing, prominence and provenance. Two researchers code the same reports blind, and we report inter-rater agreement.

Framing

Every genuine mention is coded on the four-level ladder. A count cannot tell a footprint pledge from a carbon budget.

Consistency

The Instrument also searches for activity that works against environmental limits. It tests whether conduct matches the university's own commitments, and whether it stays within the limits the sector's science establishes.

Scale

The design keeps judgement where it belongs and still covers a sector across a decade. Fully automated coding would hollow out the findings. Fully manual coding would not reach the corpus.

  • Corpus 37 Australian public universities. Annual reports and strategies, 2016 to 2025.
  • Pilot Eight universities, four environmental goals. Coding under way.
  • Reliability Dual independent coding, with inter-rater agreement reported.

05

From a pilot to a sector, then beyond it.

The grant funds two HDR research assistants to extend the validated coding to all seventeen goals and across the sector for 2025. A methods paper is under review. A findings paper follows, with both assistants named as authors.

  1. 01

    Pilot

    Eight Australian universities, four environmental goals. Environmental-goal coding completed and dual-coded.

  2. 02

    Extend

    All seventeen goals across the eight universities, then the full sector for 2025.

  3. 03

    Scale

    37 Australian public universities across a decade, built for longitudinal analysis.

  4. 04

    International

    Are there planetary universities anywhere? We welcome partners who want to find out.

Outputs: a validated Instrument, an open coding method, a sector dataset and two peer-reviewed papers.

06

A collaboration built for two kinds of judgement.

The method needs computational skill to read a decade of reports, and policy judgement to decide what each passage commits to. The team holds both.
